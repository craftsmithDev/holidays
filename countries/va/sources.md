# Vatican City: sources and verification

State: 15 November 2026. Rules: [rules.yaml](rules.yaml). The comparison with external data for 2024–2030 (generated by `node scripts/compare.mjs VA`) is at the [end of this file](#comparison-with-external-data).

The Vatican has the least settled data of the three countries. The list of days is in force and identical in three regulations, but two points (the date of the State anniversary, and whether Ascension and Corpus Christi stay on Thursday) rest on inference and are marked below.

## Sources

1. **Regolamento Generale della Curia Romana** (approved 15 April 1999, in force 1 July 1999), Art. 50, ULSA text. Holy See staff.
2. **Regolamento generale per il personale del Governatorato dello Stato della Città del Vaticano**, decree N. CXXVI of 21 November 2010, in force 1 January 2011, Art. 27. Staff of the State’s administration. It replaced the regulation of 3 May 1995, which was not found online.
3. **Regolamento del Personale della Curia Romana** of 23 November 2025, in force 1 January 2026 ad experimentum for five years, Art. 52. Replaces source 1 for Holy See staff.
4. **Code of Canon Law**, can. 1246 §1: the holy days of obligation.
5. **Vatican News**, 17 September 2025: Leo XIV’s name day, Saint Robert Bellarmine, 17 September, “neue Vatikanfeiertag”, held for the first time on that day.
6. **Regolamento dell’Opera Romana Pellegrinaggi** (ULSA Bollettino n. 9, 2000), Art. 39. Not a Vatican body (it belongs to the Vicariate of Rome), but its holiday list follows the Vatican one and gives the dates the Vatican texts leave open: “11 febbraio, Anniversario della firma dei Patti Lateranensi”, “giovedì dell’Ascensione”, “giovedì del Corpo e Sangue di Cristo”. Cited as supporting evidence only.

Sources 1–3 use the same wording: “Oltre alle domeniche e alle altre feste di precetto, saranno anche giorni di vacanza:” the anniversary of the pope’s election, his name day, the anniversary of the institution of the State, Saint Joseph the Worker, the last three days of Holy Week, Easter Monday and Tuesday, the eve and the day after the Assumption, All Souls, the eve and the two days after Christmas, the last day of the year.

## Type rule

All days are `public` (decision D-va-1): the State has no general labour act with its own list, and the personnel regulations above are the employer rules for the Holy See and the Governatorato. No `observance` days.

## How the rules were built

| Day | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Mary Mother of God, Epiphany, Saint Joseph, Saints Peter and Paul, Assumption, All Saints, Immaculate Conception, Christmas | fixed dates | can. 1246 §1 |
| Ascension, Corpus Christi | Easter +39, Easter +60 (Thursdays) | can. 1246 §1; see open points |
| Anniversary of the State | 11 February | see open points |
| Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Holy Saturday | Easter −3, −2, −1 | “i tre ultimi giorni della Settimana Santa” |
| Easter Monday, Easter Tuesday | Easter +1, +2 | regulations |
| Saint Joseph the Worker | 1 May | regulations |
| Eve and day after the Assumption | 14 and 16 August | regulations |
| All Souls | 2 November | regulations |
| Christmas Eve, the two days after Christmas, last day of the year | 24, 26, 27, 31 December | regulations |
| Election anniversary | 16 Oct 1979–2004 (John Paul II), 19 Apr 2006–2012 (Benedict XVI), 13 Mar 2014–2025 (Francis), 8 May from 2026 (Leo XIV) | dates of election; no anniversary in the year of an election or after the pope’s death or resignation |
| Name day | 4 Nov 1978–2004 (Karol: Charles Borromeo), 23 Apr 2013–2024 (Jorge: George), 17 Sep from 2025 (Robert: Robert Bellarmine) | Benedict XVI (Joseph) had 19 March, already Saint Joseph; Francis died on 21 April 2025, two days before his name day |

The rules for Leo XIV have no end year. When the see falls vacant, they get `to` and the successor’s dates are added.

## Differences with OpenHolidays (2024–2030)

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ours only: Ascension and Corpus Christi (Thursdays) | open | OpenHolidays has neither day, on Thursday or Sunday. Both are feasts of obligation under can. 1246 §1, which the regulations include. See open points. |
| Ours only: New Year’s Eve | ours | “l’ultimo giorno dell’anno” in all three regulations. |
| Ours only: name day 17 September 2025 | ours | Vatican News reports the first name-day holiday of Leo XIV on 17 September 2025. OpenHolidays starts it in 2026. |
| 2025 Holy Week and Easter week: ours 17–19 and 21–22 April; theirs Maundy Thursday 21, Good Friday 22, Easter Saturday 23, Easter Sunday 24, Easter Monday 25, Easter Tuesday 26 April | ours | Easter 2025 was 20 April. OpenHolidays shifted the whole block by four days; its own Easter Sunday falls on a Thursday. A data error on their side. |
| Theirs only: Saint George, 23 April 2025 | ours | Name day of Francis, who died on 21 April 2025. With no pope, there is no name day of the pope. Open point below. |
| Theirs only: Nativity of Mary, 8 September | ours (left out) | Not a feast of obligation (can. 1246) and not in any of the three regulations. No source found. |
| Theirs only: Easter Sunday | both | Sundays are not listed (D7). |
| Their label for 16 August is Saint Stephen of Hungary, ours “Day after the Assumption” | both | Same date; the regulations name the day by its position after the Assumption. |

date-holidays has Ascension and Corpus Christi on Thursday, like ours. A run for 2000–2023 adds two differences: date-holidays has an “election anniversary” on the election days themselves (19 April 2005, 13 March 2013). These were days of a conclave, not anniversaries; left out. All other days agree in all 24 years, including the name days of John Paul II and Francis.

## Open points

- **Ascension and Corpus Christi.** Can. 1246 §2 lets a bishops’ conference move them to Sunday; the Italian conference does. The data keeps the Thursdays: the regulations list the feasts of obligation without a date, the Opera Romana Pellegrinaggi lists the Thursdays, and date-holidays agrees. No Vatican source that moves them to Sunday was found. Italian Wikipedia gives Corpus Domini 2026 as 7 June (a Sunday). A Governatorato staff calendar would settle it.
- **Anniversary of the State.** The regulations do not give the date. 11 February is the signature of the Lateran Treaty (1929); the treaty entered into force, and the State came into being, on 7 June 1929. The data uses 11 February (Opera Romana Pellegrinaggi, OpenHolidays, date-holidays).
- **Sede vacante.** Whether the offices closed on 23 April 2025 (Francis’ name day, two days after his death) is not documented. The data says no.
- **2000–2010 for the Governatorato.** The 1995 Governatorato regulation was not found; for these years the data rests on the Curia regulation of 1999, which has the same list.
- **Latin names** are not included (decision D-va-2).
