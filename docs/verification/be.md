# Belgium: verification

State: 15 November 2026. Data: `data/be/`. Report: [report-be.md](report-be.md) (generated by `node scripts/compare.mjs BE`, years 2024–2030).

## Sources

The visibility plugin has no Belgian data, so the country was researched from official sources. All legal texts were read on ejustice (Justel and the Moniteur belge).

1. **Holidays Act** of 4 January 1974 ([Justel](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/loi/1974/01/04/1974010407/justel)). Art. 4: ten holidays a year on which employees may not work; the King fixes the dates. Art. 1: the act does not apply to the public sector (state, regions, communities, provinces, municipalities), except for industrial or commercial establishments and health care. Art. 6–9: replacement days (below).
2. **Royal Decree of 18 April 1974**, Art. 1 ([Justel FR](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/arrete/1974/04/18/1974041801/justel), Dutch version read as well): 1 January, Easter Monday, 1 May, Ascension, Whit Monday, 21 July, Assumption, All Saints, 11 November, Christmas. These ten days are `public`. The consolidated text notes that the Royal Decree of 10 February 2008 replaced Ascension by 2 May for 2008, when Ascension fell on 1 May.
3. **FPS Employment** page “Jours fériés” ([emploi.belgique.be](https://emploi.belgique.be/fr/themes/jours-feries-et-conges/jours-feries), Dutch page read as well): the same ten days with their names, and the replacement rules.
4. **Royal Decree of 19 November 1998** on leave of federal civil servants, Art. 14 ([Justel](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/arrete/1998/11/19/1998002123/justel)): federal staff are off on the ten days and on 2 November, 15 November and 26 December. When one of these days falls on a non-working day, they are off on 27, 28, 29, 30 and 31 December instead. 2 November, 15 November (King’s Feast) and 26 December are `observance`.
5. **Community days**:
   - Flemish Community: decree of 7 November 1990, Art. 6, “De feestdag van de Vlaamse Gemeenschap is 11 juli” ([Justel](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/decreet/1990/11/07/1990030433/justel)).
   - French Community: decree of 3 July 1991, Art. 1er, “La fête de la Communauté française de Belgique est célébrée chaque année le 27 septembre” (Moniteur belge of 15 November 1991, p. 25658, read in the [PDF image](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/decret/1991/07/03/1991029619/moniteur)). It repeals the decree of 20 July 1975.
   - German-speaking Community: decree of 1 October 1990, Art. 1, “Die Deutschsprachige Gemeinschaft feiert ihren Festtag jedes Jahr am 15. November” (Belgisches Staatsblatt of 15 November 1990, p. 21587, read in the [PDF image](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/decret/1990/10/01/1990030370/moniteur)).

   None of the three decrees gives a day off. They are `observance` (decision D-bnl-1).
6. **Cross-check, not a source**: OpenHolidays API, date-holidays, and the holiday list of the Royal Observatory (robinfo.oma.be, “Jours fériés belges 2026”), which shows the same ten days, the three federal days and the community and regional days for 2026.

The belgium.be holiday page answered with a captcha and was not used.

## Replacement days

Holidays Act Art. 6–9: when a holiday falls on a Sunday or on a day the company does not usually work, the employee gets a replacement day on a usual working day. The joint committee may fix it (made binding by royal decree), otherwise the works council, the union delegation, or an agreement with the employee; without any decision it is the first usual working day of the company after the holiday (Art. 9). The FPS Employment page says the same and mentions sectors that replace a day altogether (steel industry, CP 104: 1 December instead of 11 November).

The date therefore depends on the company and its working days. No `substitute` is modelled; a substitute rule would claim one date for everyone. Example: All Saints 2026 is a Sunday; many companies give Monday 2 November, others a day of their choice.

## Regions and communities

ISO 3166-2:BE has three regions (BE-BRU, BE-VLG, BE-WAL) at the first level and ten provinces below them. The regions are listed; no holiday differs by province.

The communities are not ISO subdivisions. Their days are given to the regions where the community has competence (decision D-bnl-1):

| Day | Community | Regions in the data | Note on the observance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 July | Flemish | BE-VLG, BE-BRU | In Brussels the Flemish institutions. |
| 27 September | French | BE-WAL, BE-BRU | Not in the nine German-speaking municipalities; in Brussels the French institutions. |
| 15 November | German-speaking | BE-WAL | Only the nine municipalities Amel, Büllingen, Burg-Reuland, Bütgenbach, Eupen, Kelmis, Lontzen, Raeren, Sankt Vith. |

## Differences with OpenHolidays

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ours only: 2 November, 15 November (King’s Feast), 26 December, all regions, `observance` | ours | Days off of the federal administration (AR 19 November 1998, Art. 14); not statutory for employees, so `observance`. OpenHolidays lists only the ten days. |
| Ours only: community days 11 July, 27 September, 15 November | ours | Decrees of the communities; `observance`. |
| Theirs only: Easter Sunday, Whit Sunday (public) | both | Sundays are not listed (decision D7). |

The ten statutory days agree in every year 2024–2030.

## Differences with date-holidays

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ours only: 11 July in BE-BRU; 27 September in BE-BRU | ours | Both communities have institutions in Brussels (D-bnl-1). date-holidays has 11 July only in BE-VLG and 27 September only in BE-WAL. |
| Ours only: 26 December in BE-BRU and BE-WAL | ours | Federal administration day in the whole country (AR 1998, Art. 14). date-holidays has it only in BE-VLG. |
| Theirs only: Fête de l’Iris, 8 May, BE-BRU (observance) | open | The day of the Brussels-Capital Region. No legal source was read; not added. Candidate for a Brussels `observance`. |
| Theirs only: Epiphany, Saint Nicholas (6 December), Valentine’s Day, Mother’s Day (observance) | ours (left out) | Customs, not holidays. |
| Theirs only: Easter Sunday, Whit Sunday (observance) | both | Sundays are not listed (D7). |

date-holidays agrees on the ten statutory days, 2 November, 15 November and the German-speaking Community day.

## Checked by hand

2025, 2026 and 2027 were recomputed independently from the texts above (a separate script, not the engine) and compared with `dist/json/be/`: no difference. 2026 also matches the Royal Observatory list date for date. 2008 gives 2 May for Ascension.

## Open points

- Day of the Walloon Region (third Sunday of September) and day of the Brussels-Capital Region (8 May): their legal basis was not read. The Walloon day is a Sunday and would be left out under D7 anyway.
- German names of the ten days: no official German list was found online (the FPS Employment page has no German version). The names are the usual ones in the German-speaking Community.
- Banks: Belgian banks have no statutory extra closing day that was found; none is modelled.
