# Luxembourg: sources and verification

## Sources

The visibility plugin has no Luxembourg data, so the country was researched from official sources.

1. **Code du travail**, consolidated version of 1 January 2026 ([Legilux PDF](https://data.legilux.public.lu/filestore/eli/etat/leg/code/travail/20260101/fr/pdf/eli-etat-leg-code-travail-20260101-fr-pdf.pdf)), Art. L. 232-1 to L. 232-14.
   - Art. L. 232-2 (law of 25 April 2019): eleven “jours fériés légaux”: New Year, Easter Monday, 1 May, Europe Day, Ascension, Whit Monday, the public celebration of the Grand Duke’s birthday “qui est fixé au 23 juin”, Assumption, All Saints, the first and second day of Christmas. These are `public`.
   - Art. L. 232-3 (law of 8 February 2024): a holiday on a Sunday, or two holidays on the same day, gives a compensatory day off “dans un délai de trois mois”. Art. L. 232-6 adds the same for days the employee would not have worked.
2. **Law of 10 April 1976** on legal holidays (Mémorial A 1976, n° 16, [PDF](https://data.legilux.public.lu/file/eli-etat-leg-memorial-1976-16-fr-pdf.pdf)), Art. 2: the same ten days without Europe Day. Art. 3 in its original wording moved the Grand Duke’s birthday to 24 June when 23 June was a Sunday and let the minister fix replacement days for other Sunday holidays.
3. **Parliamentary file 4608/4828** ([Chambre des Députés](https://wdocs-pub.chd.lu/docs/compilation/docpa/pdf/4608_Dossier_Complet.pdf)): the law of 12 February 1999 replaced the replacement days by a day off taken by each employee within three months; the 2002 text that followed is the one now in Art. L. 232-3. The government letter in the file names 23 June 2002 (a Sunday) as the next case of the new rule. From 2000 on, no holiday moves.
4. **Guichet.lu**, “Travail pendant les jours fériés” ([FR](https://guichet.public.lu/fr/citoyens/travail/conges-jours-feries/jours-feries/travail-jours-feries.html), German and English versions read as well): the eleven days with their French, German and English names, and: “dans le secteur bancaire, le Vendredi Saint et l’après-midi de la veille de Noël sont à considérer comme des jours fériés légaux bancaires.” These two are `bank`; Christmas Eve from 12:00.
5. **Luxembourgish names**: the Lëtzebuerger Online Dictionnaire (lod.lu, Zenter fir d’Lëtzebuerger Sprooch): Neijoerschdag, Karfreideg, Ouschterméindeg, Dag vun der Aarbecht, Europadag, Christi Himmelfaart, Päischtméindeg (LOD lists Péngschtméindeg as a variant), Nationalfeierdag, Léiffrawëschdag, Allerhellegen, Hellegowend, Chrëschtdag, Stiefesdag.
6. **Cross-check, not a source**: OpenHolidays API, date-holidays, and the list on luxembourg.public.lu (“Public holidays in Luxembourg”, dates for 2026 and 2027).

## Compensatory days

The Code gives a compensatory day off, not a fixed date: within three months, chosen per employee. No `substitute` is modelled. In 2024 Ascension and Europe Day both fell on 9 May; the data has two entries on that day, and Art. L. 232-3 gives one extra day off within three months. In the range 2000–2050 this happens again only if Ascension falls on 9 May after 2019; the next time is 2086, outside the range.

## Regions (decision D-bnl-4)

No holiday differs within Luxembourg, so `regions` is empty, as CONTRIBUTING.md asks. The 12 cantons of ISO 3166-2:LU are not listed. (Liechtenstein lists its municipalities, D14; the owner may prefer the same here.)

## Differences with OpenHolidays

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ours only: Christmas Eve afternoon (`bank` from 12:00) | ours | Guichet.lu: bank holiday under the banking collective agreement. |

Good Friday is a `Bank` day in OpenHolidays too. All eleven statutory days agree in 2024–2030.

## Checked by hand

2025, 2026 and 2027 were recomputed independently from the Code (a separate script) and compared with `json/lu/`: no difference. The dates for 2026 and 2027 on luxembourg.public.lu match. 2002 keeps National Day on Sunday 23 June; Europe Day starts in 2019.

## Open points

- The banking collective agreement itself was not read; the bank days rest on Guichet.lu. Since when Good Friday and Christmas Eve afternoon are bank holidays is not known, so they are listed for every year.
- The law of 25 April 2019 and the 2002 amending law were read through the consolidated Code and the parliamentary file, not in the Mémorial.
