# Italy: verification

State: 15 November 2026. Data: `data/it/`. Report: [report-it.md](report-it.md) (generated by `node scripts/compare.mjs IT`, years 2024–2030).

## Sources

All legal texts were read on Normattiva, in the version in force on 15 November 2026.

1. **Legge 27 maggio 1949, n. 260**, Art. 1 (2 June, festa nazionale) and Art. 2 (the giorni festivi). The Normattiva text of Art. 2 still lists Saint Joseph, Ascension, Corpus Domini, Saints Peter and Paul and 4 November. Legge 54/1977 ended these days without changing the wording of Art. 2, so the printed list is longer than the law in force.
2. **Legge 5 marzo 1977, n. 54**, Art. 1: Epiphany, Saint Joseph, Ascension, Corpus Domini and Saints Peter and Paul stop being holidays “agli effetti civili”; 2 June and 4 November move to the first Sunday of June and of November.
3. **D.P.R. 28 dicembre 1985, n. 792**, Art. 1 (in force from 1 January 1986): the religious holidays agreed with the Holy See, which restores Epiphany. It also names 29 June “per il comune di Roma”, the only patron-saint day in state law.
4. **Legge 20 novembre 2000, n. 336**, Art. 1: 2 June is again a holiday “a decorrere dal 2001”.
5. **Decreto-legge 22 febbraio 2011, n. 5**, Art. 1: 17 March 2011 (150 years of unification) is a giorno festivo for 2011 only.
6. **Legge 8 ottobre 2025, n. 151** (G.U. n. 236 of 10 October 2025), Art. 1 para. 2 adds “il 4 ottobre: festa nazionale di San Francesco d’Assisi, patrono d’Italia” to Art. 2 of Legge 260/1949; Art. 3 para. 3 puts the law in force on 1 January 2026. Verified: the first 4 October holiday is 2026 (a Sunday), the first one on a weekday is Monday 4 October 2027.
7. **South Tyrol**: the territorial agreement for commerce in the province of Bolzano of 26 September 2016, Art. 8 (“per tutto il territorio provinciale, la festività del Patrono locale, previsto dal CCNL, coincida con il lunedì di Pentecoste”), and the contract for the executives of the Region Trentino-Alto Adige and the chambers of commerce of Trento and Bolzano 2019–2021, Art. 22 (Whit Monday for offices in the province of Bolzano, the local patron saint for offices in the province of Trento).

External data (OpenHolidays, date-holidays, Wikipedia, calendar sites) was used only for the comparison below.

## Type rule

`public` = a giorno festivo under Legge 260/1949 Art. 2 as amended (sources 1–6). From 2026 that gives 12 listed days: 1 January, 6 January, Easter Monday, 25 April, 1 May, 2 June, 15 August, 4 October, 1 November, 8 December, 25 and 26 December. Easter Sunday is a Sunday and not listed (D7).

`observance` = Whit Monday in South Tyrol (decision D-it-2). It rests on collective agreements that move the patron-saint holiday of the national contracts to Whit Monday, not on state law. In practice it is a general day off in the province.

Italy has no substitute days. A holiday on a Sunday stays on the Sunday; collective agreements pay compensation, which is outside this library.

## Regions

ISO 3166-2:IT has 20 regions and, below them, provinces including the autonomous provinces Bolzano (IT-BZ) and Trento (IT-TN). The data lists the 19 regions other than Trentino-Alto Adige (IT-32) plus IT-BZ and IT-TN (decision D-it-1). Every holiday except Whit Monday is nationwide.

## Municipal and regional days left out

AL decided the scope: country and region level, no municipal holidays.

- **Patron-saint days** are municipal: Rome 29 June, Milan 7 December, Turin, Florence and Genoa 24 June, Naples 19 September, Bologna 4 October, Palermo 15 July, Bari 6 December, Cagliari 30 October, Trieste 3 November, Venice 25 April, and every other comune. They come from the national collective agreements (the patron saint of the place of work), and for Rome also from D.P.R. 792/1985. None is listed. In South Tyrol the agreements replace them with Whit Monday, which is why that one day appears at province level.
- **Sa Die de sa Sardigna** (28 April, Sardinia): L.R. Sardegna 14 settembre 1993, n. 44, Art. 1 declares 28 April the “giornata del popolo sardo”. The search found no provision that makes it a day off for employees, and a region cannot create a giorno festivo under state law. Not listed; open point.
- No other region-level statutory holiday was found.

All nationwide days agree in every year 2024–2030 apart from 4 October.

## Open points

- **Whit Monday in South Tyrol**: no state or provincial law was found. The two agreements cited are examples; a complete check would need the territorial agreements of every sector, or a statement of the Provincia autonoma di Bolzano. If a legal act exists, the type becomes `public`.
- **Sa Die de sa Sardigna**: confirm from the full text of L.R. 44/1993 or regional circulars whether 28 April is a day off for anyone (regional offices, schools). Then it could be an `observance` in IT-88.
- **IT-32**: callers using the regional code for Trentino-Alto Adige get an error (D-it-1).
- **Patron-saint days** need the municipal level (format.md, “Reserved for later”).
