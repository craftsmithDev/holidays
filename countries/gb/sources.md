# United Kingdom: sources and verification

State: 15 November 2026. Rules: [rules.yaml](rules.yaml). The comparison with external data for 2024–2030 (generated by `node scripts/compare.mjs GB`) is at the [end of this file](#comparison-with-external-data).

## Sources and method

1. **The law.** Banking and Financial Dealings Act 1971, Schedule 1, as amended ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1971/80/schedule/1)): the fixed bank holidays of England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, with the Sunday rules. The St Andrew’s Day Bank Holiday (Scotland) Act 2007 ([asp 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2007/2/enacted)) adds 30 November in Scotland from 16 January 2007, moved to the following Monday when it falls on a weekend.
2. **Royal Proclamations** under s. 1(2) and 1(3) of the 1971 Act. They add the days that Schedule 1 does not list (New Year’s Day and the early May bank holiday in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, Boxing Day and the spring bank holiday in Scotland, the Battle of the Boyne), the Saturday substitutes, and the one-off and moved days. Read in The Gazette: the 2002 Golden Jubilee (London Gazette notice L-56043-1001; Edinburgh Gazette 24921 p. 2551 for Scotland), the 2011 Royal Wedding (L-59637-1268656, E-26880-2), the 2012 Diamond Jubilee (L-59806-1377705, E-26950-1), and for the regular pattern the proclamations for 2000/2001 (L-55890-1001, L-55890-1002, L-55541-3), 2002 (L-56207-1005, Edinburgh Gazette 25013 p. 1095 and 25205 p. 961), 2003 (L-56583-1005), 2004/2005 (E-25648-1101-206, -207, L-57314-1112), 2005/2006 (L-57662-1012, E-25853-1101-280) and 2009 (L-59248-979134).
3. **gov.uk/bank-holidays** and its JSON feed `bank-holidays.json`, the official list for the three divisions. The live feed (read 15 November 2026) covers 2019–2028; an archived copy of the same feed from 2 January 2018 covers 2012–2019. The Welsh page [gov.uk/gwyliau-banc](https://www.gov.uk/gwyliau-banc) gives the Welsh names; an archived copy of 25 January 2013 gives “Jiwbilî Diemwnt y Frenhines”.
4. **gov.scot** “Scottish bank holiday dates” for the 2026 World Cup bank holiday.
5. **Cross-check, not a source**: OpenHolidays API and the npm package date-holidays.

Raw copies of the feeds and pages are in `_logger/holidays/gb-ie/` outside the repository.

## Type: bank holidays are `public`

UK law has no list of statutory days off. The bank holidays are the general public holidays, but “Your employer does not have to give you paid leave on bank or public holidays” (gov.uk). The format’s own type table names UK bank holidays as its example of `public`. In this file `public` means: a bank holiday in that part of the UK (Schedule 1, a proclamation, or the 2007 Act), plus Christmas Day and Good Friday in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, which are customary holidays there and on the gov.uk list. Every observance carries this reading in `rules.yaml`. `bank` and `observance` are not used. Decision D-gb-1.

## Second pass: years before 2012

No gov.uk feed exists for 2000–2011. The Gazette proclamations listed above were checked against the data date by date (51 cases, `isHoliday` per region): the 2002 Golden Jubilee and the spring bank holiday 2002 on Tuesday 4 June instead of Monday 27 May, in all four parts (Scotland had no 27 May 2002 either: its 2001 proclamation appointed only 26 December 2001, the November 2000 one appointed 3 and 4 June 2002); the Royal Wedding on 29 April 2011 in all parts; the substitutes of the new year 2000 (3 January, and 4 January in Scotland), of Christmas 2004 (27 and 28 December; 4 January 2005 in Scotland), Christmas 2005 (27 December) and Boxing Day 2009 (28 December); the regular early May, spring and Boxing Day proclamations 2000–2006 and 2010. No mismatch.

The Scottish proclamation for 2005 appoints 4 January “in place of Saturday, 1st January 2005”, while Schedule 1 makes 3 January the day for 2 January on a Sunday. Our chain gives 3 January to New Year’s Day and 4 January to 2 January. Same dates, other labels, as in 2023 above.

## Modelling

- **Substitutes.** Every holiday that can fall on a weekend carries `substitute: {when: {weekday: [sat, sun]}, avoidWeekdays: [sat, sun]}`. The chain gives Monday 27 and Tuesday 28 December when Christmas is a Saturday, and Monday 3 and Tuesday 4 January in Scotland when New Year’s Day is a Saturday. The weekend date stays in the list (D10), although the Act makes only the weekday a bank holiday; consumers that need the day off filter on weekdays or on `substituteFor`. Decision D-gb-3.
- **Moved days.** The early May bank holiday 2020 (8 May, VE Day) and the spring bank holiday 2002, 2012 and 2022 are `list` rules inside the date rule, valid only in that year.
- **One-off days.** Golden Jubilee 2002, Royal Wedding 2011, Diamond Jubilee 2012, Platinum Jubilee 2022, State Funeral 2022, Coronation 2023, and the Scottish World Cup bank holiday on 15 June 2026.
- **Scotland.** 2 January, Good Friday, no Easter Monday, summer bank holiday on the first Monday in August (observance-level `date`), St Andrew’s Day from 2007.
- **Northern Ireland.** St Patrick’s Day and the Battle of the Boyne, both with substitutes (gov.uk: 18 March 2019 and 2024, 19 March 2018; 13 July 2020 and 2026, 14 July 2025).
- **Wales.** Same as England.
- **Names.** English from gov.uk; Welsh (`cy`) from gov.uk/gwyliau-banc for every day except the Golden Jubilee 2002 and the Royal Wedding 2011, for which no official Welsh name was found. No official Scottish Gaelic name was found for any day, so there are none (decision D-gb-2).

## Differences with OpenHolidays

OpenHolidays has no data for the United Kingdom: the API returns an empty list for `GB`, and `GB` is not in its country list (`/Countries`, read 15 November 2026). Every entry in the report is “ours only” for that reason. No action.

## Differences with date-holidays

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ours only: World Cup bank holiday, 15 June 2026, Scotland | ours | gov.uk feed and gov.scot: approved by the King on the proposal of the First Minister. date-holidays predates it. |
| Theirs only: Easter Sunday, Mother’s Day, Father’s Day (observance) | both | Sundays are not listed (D7); Mother’s and Father’s Day are not holidays. |

All other dates agree, including the substitutes and the weekend originals.

## Open points

- Scottish Gaelic names: no official source found (gov.scot publishes the bank holiday page in English only). Add `gd` names when a Scottish Government or Bòrd na Gàidhlig source is found.
- Welsh names for the Golden Jubilee 2002 and the Royal Wedding 2011.
- Local holidays in Scotland (set by councils, per gov.scot) are not modelled; they need the municipal level.
- Banks in Scotland often close on the English dates instead of the Scottish ones (gov.scot). Not modelled; there is no legal rule to model.
