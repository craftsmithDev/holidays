# Ireland: sources and verification

State: 15 November 2026. Rules: [rules.yaml](rules.yaml). The comparison with external data for 2024–2030 (generated by `node scripts/compare.mjs IE`) is at the [end of this file](#comparison-with-external-data).

## Sources

1. **Organisation of Working Time Act 1997**, Second Schedule para. 1, in the revised version of the Law Reform Commission, updated to 20 January 2025 ([revisedacts.lawreform.ie](https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/1997/act/20/revised/en/html)); the enacted text on irishstatutebook.ie has the same list. Public holidays: Christmas Day, St Stephen’s Day, St Patrick’s Day, Easter Monday, the first Monday in May, June and August, the last Monday in October, 1 January, and days prescribed under para. 1(g). Section 21 sets the entitlement.
2. **S.I. No. 50 of 2022** (Covid-19 Commemoration Regulations): 18 March 2022 once; from 2023 the first Monday in February, or 1 February when that is a Friday.
3. **S.I. No. 419 of 2001** (National Day of Mourning Regulations): Friday 14 September 2001. The revised Act lists it in its annotation E139 with the date misprinted as 14.09.2011; the S.I. itself says 2001.
4. **Workplace Relations Commission**, “Public Holidays”: the ten days, the 2026 dates, Good Friday not a public holiday, and the weekend rule.
5. **FSSU** (Financial Support Services Unit, funded by the Department of Education), financial guidelines “Public Holidays” for 2022, 2025 and 2026, each in English and Irish. They are the source of the Irish names and of the English names of the Monday holidays.
6. **Cross-check, not a source**: OpenHolidays API and the npm package date-holidays.

citizensinformation.ie and gov.ie answered HTTP 403 to automated requests on 15 November 2026 and are not cited.

## Modelling

- **Type.** Every day is `public`: a public holiday under Schedule 2. The Act gives a choice to the employer (paid day off, paid day off within a month, an extra day of annual leave, or an extra day’s pay; s. 21), so `public` here means “public holiday under the Act”, which is how Irish law defines the term.
- **No substitute days.** Schedule 2 has no weekend rule. The WRC: “Where the public holiday falls on a day which is not a normal working day for that business (e.g. a Saturday or Sunday) employees still have entitlement to benefit … However, there is no legal entitlement to have the next working day off work.” So no `substitute`. Decision D-ie-1.
- **St Brigid’s Day.** `nthWeekday` first Monday in February, with `adjust` −3 days when that Monday is 4 February (which happens exactly when 1 February is a Friday). From 2023. Checked: 6 February 2023 (S.I. explanatory note), 3 February 2025 and 2 February 2026 (FSSU, WRC), 1 February 2030 (a Friday).
- **Church holidays** (Schedule 2 para. 3 and 4: an employer may give a nearby church holiday instead) are not listed. They change nothing for a calendar unless the employer chooses them.
- **Names.** Irish names from the FSSU guideline, except Saint Brigid’s Day: the FSSU tables call it “First Monday in February” / “An chéad Luan i mí Feabhra”, which is wrong in years when 1 February is a Friday, so the data uses the name of the S.I. explanatory note (“St. Brigid’s day”) and its Irish form “Lá Fhéile Bríde” from the Irish text of the FSSU guideline 2022 and from téarma.ie, the national terminology database. The Act names the four Monday holidays only by their rule, and so do the WRC and the FSSU (“First Monday in May”, “An chéad Luan i mí na Bealtaine”); the data uses those names. For 18 March 2022 the FSSU name “Extra public holiday” / “Lá saoire poiblí breise” is used. For 14 September 2001 the English name is the title of the S.I.; no official Irish name was found, so the Irish name is the generic “Lá saoire poiblí breise” with a note.

## Open points

- Bank closing days (Good Friday, weekday after a weekend public holiday): candidates for `type: bank` once an official source (Central Bank of Ireland, Banking & Payments Federation Ireland) is found.
- An official Irish name for the National Day of Mourning (14 September 2001).
- The commonly used English names (“May bank holiday”, “June bank holiday”, “August bank holiday”, “October bank holiday”) were not found in an official source that could be read; the data keeps the descriptive names of the WRC and the FSSU.
