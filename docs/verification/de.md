# Germany: verification

State: 15 November 2026. Data: `data/de/`. Report: [report-de.md](report-de.md) (generated by `node scripts/compare.mjs DE`, years 2024–2030). A second run over 2016–2023 was read as well; its differences are covered below.

## Sources and method

1. **Federal law.** Only the Day of German Unity is federal: Einigungsvertrag Art. 2 para. 2 ([gesetze-im-internet.de](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/einigvtr/art_2.html)). The Federal Ministry of the Interior ([Nationale Feiertage](https://www.bmi.bund.de/DE/themen/verfassung/staatliche-symbole/nationale-feiertage/nationale-feiertage-node.html)) confirms that all other holidays are Land law and that nine days are holidays in every Land.
2. **The 16 Feiertagsgesetze**, each read in its consolidated version on the official portal of the Land (juris portals rendered in a browser, BRAVORS, REVOSax, recht.nrw.de, NI-VORIS, gesetze-bayern.de, Transparenzportal Bremen). For each law the list of holidays and its amendment history were read; earlier versions were opened where a holiday was added or removed after 2000.
3. **Gazettes and ordinances** for the changes: Reformation Day 2017 in eleven Länder, Reformation Day from 2018 in Bremen, Hamburg, Lower Saxony and Schleswig-Holstein, International Women’s Day in Berlin (2019) and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania (2023), World Children’s Day in Thuringia (2019), Berlin’s one-off days 2020, 2025 and 2028.
4. **Partial-territory days** (decision D6): Bavaria FTG Art. 1 para. 1 no. 2, para. 2 and para. 3; the Saxon FronleichnamsVO; ThürFGtG § 2 para. 2 and § 10.
5. **Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve**: AZV § 6 (federal civil servants) and LadSchlG § 3 (shops close at 14:00 on 24 December).
6. **Cross-check, not a source**: OpenHolidays API and date-holidays.

## Classification

`public` means a gesetzlicher Feiertag for the whole Land. Every Land law makes these days of general rest from work. Days that a law protects in another way (church holidays with a right to stay away from work, quiet days, Maundy Thursday) are not listed.

`observance` covers three cases:

| Holiday | Land | Why not `public` |
|---|---|---|
| Augsburg Peace Festival, 8 August | BY | Gesetzlicher Feiertag only in the city of Augsburg (FTG Art. 1 para. 2). |
| Assumption Day, 15 August | BY | Gesetzlicher Feiertag only in municipalities with a mainly Catholic population (FTG Art. 1 para. 1 no. 2); the Bavarian statistics office determines them from the census (para. 3). |
| Corpus Christi | SN | Only in the municipalities listed in the FronleichnamsVO (Sorbian Catholic area, today Landkreis Bautzen). |
| Corpus Christi | TH | Only in municipalities with a mainly Catholic population (ThürFGtG § 2 para. 2); until an ordinance exists, where it was a holiday in 1994 (§ 10 para. 1). No ordinance was found. |
| Christmas Eve, New Year’s Eve | all | Not a Feiertag in any Land. Off for federal civil servants (AZV § 6), the same approach as the LLV calendar for Liechtenstein (D13). |

Assumption Day is `public` in Saarland, where SFG § 2 lists it for the whole Land. Corpus Christi is `public` in BW, BY, HE, NW, RP and SL.

## Differences with OpenHolidays

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ours only: Augsburg Peace Festival (BY) | ours, as observance | City of Augsburg only (FTG Art. 1 para. 2). OpenHolidays has no city level. |
| Ours only: Corpus Christi in SN and TH | ours, as observance | Statutory only in parts of the Land (D6). A default query (`public`) does not return them. |
| Ours only: Christmas Eve, New Year’s Eve | ours, as observance | No Feiertag; days off for federal civil servants (AZV § 6). |
| Theirs only: Easter Sunday and Whit Sunday in BB | both | Brandenburg’s FTG § 2 lists both. They are Sundays and left out (D7). |
| 2016–2019: every day is ours only | ours | OpenHolidays returns no German data before 2020. |

All other days agree in 2020–2030, including Assumption Day in BY, Berlin’s 8 May 2025 and 17 June 2028, and Women’s Day in MV from 2023.

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|

| Theirs only: Reformation Day 2017 in HH | probably theirs | Hamburg declared 31 October 2017 a holiday by ordinance under FeiertG HA § 2 para. 1 no. 1, according to a news report of evangelisch.de of 30 April 2013 on the Senate decision. | Theirs only: Day of Repentance and Prayer as observance outside Saxony | ours (left out) | Statutory only in Saxony. Baden-Württemberg lists it as a church holiday (FTG § 2) with a right to stay away from work, not a day off. |

| Theirs only: Advent Sundays, Mother’s Day, Valentine’s Day, Totensonntag, Volkstrauertag | ours (left out) | Sundays or commemorations without a day off (D7). |

Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve agree (date-holidays has them as observances too).

## Second pass: dates 2025, 2026, 2027

Checked again, independently of the first pass, per Land:

- **Easter-based days.** Easter Sunday 2025-04-20, 2026-04-05, 2027-03-28 as published in the official holiday calendar of the City of Vienna ([ICS](https://www.wien.gv.at/spezial/daten/ics/feiertage.ics)); the Gregorian Easter date is the same in Germany. Good Friday, Easter Monday, Ascension, Whit Monday and Corpus Christi follow at −2, +1, +39, +50 and +60 days. Our dates: 2025-04-18, 04-21, 05-29, 06-09, 06-19; 2026-04-03, 04-06, 05-14, 05-25, 06-04; 2027-03-26, 03-29, 05-06, 05-17, 05-27. All match.
- **Day of Repentance and Prayer** (SN). BW FTG § 2 defines it as the Wednesday before the last Sunday of the church year. The first Sunday of Advent falls on 30 November 2025, 29 November 2026 and 28 November 2027, so the day is 19 November 2025, 18 November 2026 and 17 November 2027. Our dates match.
- **Fixed dates and which Land has which day** were compared again against the holiday list of each Land law (sources above). No difference.
- Berlin’s 8 May 2025 is in the version of FeiertG BE valid until 08.05.2025 and in the data.

No date had to be corrected.

## Open points

- **Hamburg, Reformation Day 2017.** The Senate ordinance under FeiertG HA § 2 was not found in an official source. date-holidays has the day. Add it with a source once the HmbGVBl. reference is confirmed.
- **Corpus Christi in Thuringia.** ThürFGtG § 10 keeps the 1994 status until an ordinance under § 2 para. 2 is issued. Whether such an ordinance exists was not confirmed.
- **Municipal level.** The Augsburg Peace Festival, Assumption Day in Bavaria and Corpus Christi in Saxony and Thuringia need the municipal level (format.md, “Reserved for later”). The Bavarian list of municipalities with a Catholic majority is published by the Landesamt für Statistik.
- **Easter Sunday and Whit Sunday in Brandenburg** are Feiertage under FTG § 2. They are left out under D7; a consumer who needs them can add them.
- **Christmas Eve before 2006.** AZV § 6 is from 2006; the earlier federal rule was not checked. The observance is listed for all years.
