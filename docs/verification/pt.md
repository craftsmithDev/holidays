# Portugal: verification

State: 15 November 2026. Data: `data/pt/`. Report: [report-pt.md](report-pt.md) (generated by `node scripts/compare.mjs PT`, years 2024–2030).

## Scope

Country and the two autonomous regions. Municipal holidays (feriado municipal, one day per municipality, Labour Code Art. 235) are out of scope by the owner's decision: the library covers country and region only. OpenHolidays lists them as optional days per municipality; they are not compared.

Regions are the 18 districts (PT-01 to PT-18) and the autonomous regions Açores (PT-20) and Madeira (PT-30), the first-level ISO 3166-2:PT subdivisions. The districts have no holidays of their own; they are listed so that a district code is accepted and gets the national days (decision D-pt-1).

## Sources

1. **Labour Code, national holidays.** Read in the official gazette PDFs on files.dre.pt:
   - Lei n.º 99/2003 (Código do Trabalho 2003), Art. 208 (mandatory) and Art. 209 (optional: Carnival, municipal holiday). In force from 1 December 2003.
   - Lei n.º 7/2009 (Código do Trabalho 2009), Art. 234 and Art. 235. Same list.
   - Lei n.º 23/2012, Art. 2 and Art. 10 n.º 1: Corpus Christi, 5 October, 1 November and 1 December are removed from Art. 234 with effect from 1 January 2013.
   - Lei n.º 8/2016, Art. 2 and Art. 3: the four days are restored; in force on 2 April 2016, before the first of them that year (Corpus Christi, 26 May).
2. **Azores.** Decreto Regional n.º 13/80/A (DR I Série n.º 192, 21 August 1980, p. 2305, scanned page read): the Monday of the Holy Spirit (segunda-feira do Espírito Santo, i.e. Whit Monday) is the Day of the Autonomous Region and a regional holiday.
3. **Madeira.**
   - Decreto Legislativo Regional n.º 18/2002/M (JORAM I Série n.º 138, 19 November 2002), Art. 1: 26 December is a holiday in Madeira. Its preamble names Decreto Regional n.º 27/79/M as the act that made 1 July the regional holiday.
   - DLR n.º 3/2004/M, Art. 7, and DLR n.º 39/2012/M, Art. 4 (in force 1 January 2013): 1 July (Dia da Região e das Comunidades Madeirenses) and 26 December are regional holidays in addition to the Labour Code days. The 2012 text kept both during the national suspension 2013–2015.
   - DLR n.º 17/2024/M (DR 1.ª série n.º 243, 16 December 2024), Art. 1: 2 April is the holiday of Autonomy Day (Dia da Autonomia) in Madeira; in force the day after publication, so first observed in 2025.
4. **Cross-check, not a source:** OpenHolidays API and the npm package date-holidays.

### Types

`public`: the mandatory days of the Labour Code and the regional days of the Azores and Madeira decrees. `observance`: Carnival (Shrove Tuesday), an optional holiday that is a day off only where a collective agreement or the contract grants it.

### Modelled as

- Corpus Christi, Republic Day (5 October), All Saints (1 November) and Restoration of Independence (1 December): two observances each, `to: 2012` and `from: 2016`. No entry in 2013, 2014, 2015.
- Azores Day: `easter` offset 50, region PT-20. In 2019 and 2030 it falls on 10 June together with Portugal Day; both entries are in the output.
- Madeira: Autonomy Day `from: 2025`; 26 December `from: 2002`; Madeira Day every year.
- Easter Sunday is a mandatory holiday in Art. 234 but always a Sunday and not listed (D7).

## Differences with OpenHolidays

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ours only: Azores Day (PT-20), Madeira Day and 26 December (PT-30), 2024–2030 | both agree | OpenHolidays has the same dates, but under its own subdivision codes PT-AC and PT-MA (ISO codes PT-20 and PT-30 only in a separate field). The compare script matches by code, so they show up as differences. Checked by hand: every date matches, including Azores Day 2030 on 10 June. |
| Ours only: Autonomy Day, 2 April 2025–2030 (PT-30) | ours | DLR 17/2024/M. OpenHolidays does not have it yet. |
| Theirs only: Easter Sunday | both | Sundays are not listed (D7). |
| Theirs: municipal holidays (optional, by municipality) | out of scope | Their municipal codes (PT-LE-OB …) are not regions here and are filtered out by the script. |

OpenHolidays has Carnival as optional and nationwide; ours is an observance. That counts as a match in the report.

## Differences with date-holidays

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ours only: Azores Day, Madeira Day, 26 December, Autonomy Day | ours | date-holidays has no Portuguese regions; the region queries return the national list. |
| Theirs only: Christmas Eve, New Year's Eve (observance) | ours (left out) | Neither is in Art. 234 or Art. 235. The government often gives its staff the afternoon or day off (tolerância de ponto) by a yearly order; that is not a holiday for employers. |
| Theirs only: Mother's Day, Easter Sunday | both | Sundays are not listed (D7); Mother's Day is not a holiday. |
| 2013–2015 (outside the report years): theirs has Corpus Christi, 5 October, 1 November, 1 December | ours | Lei 23/2012 Art. 10 n.º 1 removed them from 1 January 2013; Lei 8/2016 restored them in 2016. |

For 2000–2050 nationwide there is no other difference with date-holidays (checked with a script outside the report).

## Open points

- Before 1 December 2003 the holidays came from Decreto-Lei n.º 874/76. The DRE pages need JavaScript and the decree was not read; the data assumes the list the 2003 code carried over (the 2003 code names DL 874/76 among the laws it replaces). Affects 2000–2003.
- DLR 21/2009/M (Madeira's adaptation of the 2009 code) was not read. DLR 39/2012/M revoked its holiday article and restated the same two days, so the days 2009–2012 rest on DLR 3/2004/M and 18/2002/M.
- Decreto Regional n.º 27/79/M (Madeira Day) was not read; it is cited from the preamble of DLR 18/2002/M.
- Good Friday may be observed on another day of local significance in the Easter period (Art. 234 n.º 2). Such local moves are municipal and not modelled.
- Bank holidays (TARGET2, Banco de Portugal) were not researched.
