## Sources and method

A search of the BOE for these titles on 15 November 2026 found one resolution for every year from 2000 to 2027; the 2027 resolution was published on 13 October 2026. How the tables were read:

- **HTML table** (13 years): the table of the BOE HTML page, parsed cell by cell.
- **PDF text layer** (8 years): word coordinates of the official PDF, assigned to the 19 columns. The same parser run on five HTML years (2013, 2014, 2015, 2018, 2019) gave exactly the HTML tables.
- **Scan** (2000–2006): the tables are images. Each page was rendered at 200 dpi and read by hand; a script that counts the asterisks per cell gave a second reading where the scan was clean (2000, 2002). For 2006 the corrected table of BOE-A-2005-17504 was used; it agrees with the first one.
- After reading, every community has 12 days per year (11 in the Canary Islands from 2006, plus the island day), or 13 where the BOE note names an extra recuperable day. Each year was checked against these counts.

The codes of the table: `*` national holiday that cannot be replaced, `**` national holiday the community kept, `***` holiday of the community. All three are `public` here. The type of the code is not stored.

## Modelling

- **One id per holiday, one observance per group of years and regions.** A day a community moves (for example “Lunes siguiente a Todos los Santos”) keeps the id of the holiday and gets a `date` list for that year, as in docs/format.md (“Spain: holidays set by decree each year”). Example: es.all-saints 2026 is 2 November in AN, AR, AS, CL, CM, CN, EX, MD, NC and absent elsewhere, because 1 November 2026 is a Sunday and the BOE lists it nowhere.
- **Sundays.** The BOE table leaves out a national holiday that falls on a Sunday unless a community moves it. The data does the same (D7).
- **Recuperable days.** A day the BOE marks recuperable (FR code, shaded cell, or a note such as “tendrá el carácter de recuperable”) stays `public`, with a note. Catalonia often makes one day out of four recuperable at the employer’s choice; those four days carry a note in that year. Decision D-es-2.
- **Island days of the Canary Islands.** From 2006 the BOE note lists one day per island (El Hierro, Fuerteventura, Gran Canaria, La Gomera, Lanzarote with La Graciosa, La Palma, Tenerife). Each island has an id (`es.island-…`), type `observance` at ES-CN with a note naming the island, as for days that hold only in part of a region (D6). The notes 2000–2005 name no island days.
- **Arán.** The Catalan order replaces one day by 17 June (Fiesta de Arán) in the territory of Arán: 26 December in 2017, 2019, 2020, 2022–2026, Easter Monday in 2021 and 2027; in 2018 17 June was a Sunday and nothing was replaced. `es.aran-day` is an `observance` at ES-CT; the replaced day stays `public` for Catalonia with a note. Decision D-es-3.
- **Years without a resolution.** Before 2000 nothing is returned (not researched). From 2028 the library returns only the nine national holidays that the communities cannot replace (RD 2001/1983 Art. 45.1 a–c: 1 January, Good Friday, 1 May, 15 August, 12 October, 1 November, 6 December, 8 December, 25 December), in every community, and leaves them out when they fall on a Sunday, because the resolution of that year decides whether and where they move. Epiphany, Maundy Thursday, Saint Joseph or Saint James and all regional days are not returned for 2028 onwards. When the 2028 resolution is published, its table is added and the 2028 rows replace this fallback (`from: 2028` becomes `from: 2029`). Decision D-es-4.
- **Languages.** `languages: [es]`. Galician (`gl`) and Basque (`eu`) names are added from the regional calendar decrees for 2026 and 2027 (DOG Decreto 46/2025 and 68/2026, BOPV 82/2025 and 90/2026, Basque versions). The Catalan order EMT/66/2025 lists only dates; it names “Sant Esteve” and “Festa d’Aran”, and only these two have a `ca` name. Decision D-es-1.
- **Local holidays** (two per municipality) are out of scope.

## Second pass: 2025, 2026, 2027

Each of the three years was read a second time from the PDF, independently of the HTML table: page 1 with the coordinate parser, page 2 by hand from the text layout (`pdftotext -layout`), column by column for all 19 communities and cities. No difference to the data. In addition, five regional calendars were checked against the data with their own gazette text: Galicia 2026 and 2027 (DOG), País Vasco 2026 and 2027 (BOPV), Cataluña 2026 (DOGC). All match.

A round trip over the whole range compares the library output (JS runtime, every type) with the transcribed tables: 6558 region-days for 2000–2027, no difference.

## Open points

- **Empty rows.** The tables of 2004 (“26 Lunes sig. a Santiago Apóstol”) and 2010 (“26 Lunes siguiente a Santiago Apóstol”) have a row without any mark. Nothing is listed for that day. Possibly a community was meant; no correction was published.
- **Changes after publication.** A community may change its calendar after the BOE resolution (for example by a later decree). Only BOE corrections are applied. Communities that publish such changes only in their own gazette are not tracked.
- **Catalan names.** Official Catalan names for Catalonia, the Valencian Community and the Balearic Islands were not found in an official text that names the days (the DOGC order lists dates only). The DOGC text was read from a copy hosted by UGT de Catalunya, because the DOGC portal did not answer.
- **Scanned years.** 2000–2006 were read by hand. The day counts per community match the expected 12 or 13, but a misread `**`/`***` code would not change any date, and a misread column would show in the counts only if a day moved to another community. A second reader for these seven years would help.
- **Arán and D6.** See D-es-3: the replaced Catalan day stays `public` for the whole community.
