# Monaco: verification

State: 15 November 2026. Data: `data/mc/`. Report: [report-mc.md](report-mc.md) (generated by `node scripts/compare.mjs MC`, years 2024–2030).

## Sources

1. **Loi n° 798 du 18 février 1966** portant fixation des jours fériés légaux ([Legimonaco](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/loi/1966/02-18-798/)). Art. 1er lists twelve days: the Prince’s Day, New Year’s Day, Sainte-Dévote, Easter Monday, 1 May, Ascension, Whit Monday, Corpus Christi (Fête-Dieu), Assumption, All Saints, Immaculate Conception and Christmas. Para. 2 is the Sunday rule for six of them. Legimonaco shows no amendment of the article.
2. **Loi n° 800 du 18 février 1966** art. 2 ([Legimonaco](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/loi/1966/02-18-800/)): these days are compulsory paid days off for all employees. All twelve are `public`.
3. **Government page** “Public Holidays” on monentreprise.gouv.mc (updated 3 March 2026): the dates of the fixed days (Sainte-Dévote 27 January, Prince’s Day 19 November), the rule “8th December or 9th if 8th is on Sunday” and the 2026 list.
4. **Yearly circulars** in the Journal de Monaco listing the jours chômés et payés: n° 2018-12 (for 2019), n° 2023-14 (for 2024), n° 2024-7 (for 2025), n° 2025-8 (for 2026), n° 2026-12 (for 2027).
5. **Cross-check, not a source**: OpenHolidays API, date-holidays, and the FEDEM legal note “Les jours fériés en Principauté” (Monaco Business News n° 88, October 2024), which reports a circular n° 2019-1 moving 8 December 2019 to Monday 9 December.

Decisions: D-mc-1 (Sunday rule as a substitute day) and D-mc-2 (Immaculate Conception moves to Monday), in [decisions.md](../decisions.md). No regions: the holidays do not differ within Monaco.

## Differences with OpenHolidays

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| All Saints 2026 and Prince’s Day 2028 on a Sunday: theirs only the Monday, ours the Sunday and the Monday | ours | Loi n° 798 art. 1er para. 2: the Sunday is a jour férié and “le lundi qui suit sera jour férié légal” (D-mc-1). Both dates match on the Monday. |
| Assumption 2027 (Sunday): theirs no Monday 16 August | ours | Same rule; circular n° 2026-12 lists “Dimanche 15 août 2027, reporté au Lundi 16 août 2027”. |
| Immaculate Conception 2024 and 2030 (Sunday): theirs 8 December, ours 9 December | ours | Circular n° 2023-14 lists “Dimanche 8 décembre 2024, reporté au Lundi 9 décembre 2024”; the government page gives the rule (D-mc-2). |

The year files were compared day by day with the three circulars:

- 2025 (n° 2024-7): 1 January, 27 January, 21 April, 1 May, 29 May, 9 June, 19 June, 15 August, 1 November (a Saturday, no extra day), 19 November, 8 December, 25 December. Match.
- 2026 (n° 2025-8): 1 January, 27 January, 6 April, 1 May, 14 May, 25 May, 4 June, 15 August, 1 November with Monday 2 November, 19 November, 8 December, 25 December. Match.
- 2027 (n° 2026-12): 1 January, 27 January, 29 March, 1 May, 6 May, 17 May, 27 May, 15 August with Monday 16 August, 1 November, 19 November, 8 December, 25 December. Match.

2024 also matches circular n° 2023-14 (8 December moved to 9 December). No difference was found, so nothing was changed in the second pass.

## Open points

- **Prince’s Day.** The law names the feast day of the reigning Prince, not a date. It is 19 November under Rainier III and Albert II; a new reign could change it.
- **Immaculate Conception before 2019.** The move to 9 December is confirmed for 2024 by the circular and for 2019 by the FEDEM note. The Sundays 8 December 2002 and 2013 were not checked against their circulars; the data applies the same rule.
- **Sunday rule as substitute or move.** The law adds the Monday; the circulars write “reporté au lundi”. The data keeps both days (D-mc-1). A consumer who wants only days off on working days can drop the Sunday.
