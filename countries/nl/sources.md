# Netherlands: sources and verification

State: 15 November 2026. Rules: [rules.yaml](rules.yaml). The comparison with external data for 2024–2030 (generated by `node scripts/compare.mjs NL`) is at the [end of this file](#comparison-with-external-data).

## Sources

The visibility plugin has no Dutch data, so the country was researched from official sources.

1. **Algemene termijnenwet**, Art. 3 ([wetten.overheid.nl](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0002448/2010-10-10)). Para. 1: the “algemeen erkende feestdagen” are New Year’s Day, Easter Monday and Whit Monday, both Christmas days, Ascension, the day on which the King’s birthday is celebrated, and 5 May. Para. 2: Good Friday counts as one of them for this act. Art. 5a: the act also applies in Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba. Art. 3 was last changed in 1982 (version history on wetten.overheid.nl), so the list holds for 2000–2050.
2. **Rijksoverheid**, “Wanneer zijn de officiële feestdagen in Nederland?” ([page](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/arbeidsovereenkomst-en-cao/vraag-en-antwoord/officiele-feestdagen)): the dates for 2026 and 2027, and “Er is geen wet die heeft vastgelegd dat bepaalde feestdagen vrije dagen zijn voor werknemers”; the cao or the contract decides. The question pages on Good Friday, Liberation Day and King’s Day say who is usually off: most employees work on Good Friday; not everyone is off on 5 May; most companies give King’s Day off.
3. **King’s Day**: decree of 30 January 2013 ([Staatscourant 2013, 4075](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2013-4075.pdf), BWBR0032908). 27 April from 2014, “behoudens uitzonderingen in verband met zondagen”; the explanatory note says the day is marked on the day before when 27 April is a Sunday (first in 2014). The decree withdraws the Royal Decree of 24 April 1980, nr. 11 (Queen’s Day on 30 April).
4. **Queen’s Day on a Sunday**: the 1980 decree was not found online. Two Staatscourant notices show the Saturday: the airspace closure for the Queen’s visit to Leiden “op zaterdag 29 april 2000” ([Stcrt. 2000, 102](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2000-102-p9-SC24161.pdf)) and the “Regeling tijdelijke sluiting luchtruim Koninginnedag 2006” for 29 April 2006 ([Stcrt. 2006, 87](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2006-87-p21-SC75027.pdf)). The data moves 30 April to 29 April when it is a Sunday, which only happens in 2000 and 2006 within 2000–2013.
5. **Arbeidswet 2000 BES**, Art. 22–23 ([wetten.overheid.nl](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0028202/), every version since 10 October 2010 read): the holidays of the Caribbean Netherlands, paid, and paid double when worked. Changes to Art. 23 found in the versions: Dia di Rincon for Bonaire from 2020; carnival day dropped for Bonaire in 2020 and for Sint Eustatius in 2022; 1 July for Sint Eustatius from 2022; “Koningin” became “Koning” in 2013.
6. **Regeling feestdagen ambtenaren BES** ([Staatscourant 2019, 63152](https://www.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2019-63152.pdf)): names and dates for the island days (Dia di Rincon 30 April, Dia di Himno y Bandera 6 September, Statia Day 16 November, Saba Day the first Friday of December).
7. **Cross-check, not a source**: OpenHolidays API and date-holidays.

## Classification (decision D-bnl-2)

No Dutch law gives employees a day off on a holiday. The Algemene termijnenwet decides only time limits. The data still needs a line between `public` and `observance`, and draws it like this:

- `public`: the days of Art. 3 para. 1 that are a general day off in practice: New Year’s Day, Easter Monday, King’s Day (Queen’s Day until 2013), Ascension, Whit Monday, Christmas Day, Second Day of Christmas.
- `observance`: Good Friday (Art. 3 para. 2, time limits only; most employees work) and Liberation Day.

Liberation Day is in Art. 3 para. 1, but Rijksoverheid says that not everyone is off and points to the cao. Many cao’s give the day off only every fifth year (2025, 2030, …), some every year, some never. By D4 a day that is a day off only for some employees is an `observance`; a lustrum rule in the data would claim more than any law says. The note on the holiday says this. Anyone whose cao has the lustrum rule can filter by year.

## Caribbean Netherlands (decision D-bnl-3)

ISO 3166-2:NL lists Bonaire (NL-BQ1), Saba (NL-BQ2) and Sint Eustatius (NL-BQ3) as special municipalities. They are listed as regions. Aruba, Curaçao and Sint Maarten (NL-AW, NL-CW, NL-SX) are also in ISO 3166-2:NL, but they are countries of the Kingdom with their own labour law and their own ISO 3166-1 codes; they are left out and can be added as countries later.

Holidays there follow the Arbeidswet 2000 BES, Art. 23, in force since 10 October 2010. Before that date the islands belonged to the Netherlands Antilles, so the data has no days for them before 2010 (the 2010 days after 10 October, Statia Day, Saba Day and Christmas, are included). One consequence: before 2010 no entry applies in every region, so `holidays('NL', 2005)` without a region returns nothing (D8). Ask with a province.

From 2011:

| Day | European Netherlands | Caribbean Netherlands |
|---|---|---|
| Good Friday | observance | public (Art. 23 c) |
| Labour Day | not listed | public; on Monday 2 May when 1 May is a Sunday (Art. 23 para. 2) |
| Liberation Day | observance | observance (Termijnenwet only) |
| Whit Monday | public | observance (Termijnenwet only; Art. 23 lists Whit Sunday) |
| Dia di Rincon, 30 April | – | public in Bonaire since 2020 |
| Emancipation Day, 1 July | – | public in Sint Eustatius since 2022 |
| Dia di Himno y Bandera, 6 September | – | public in Bonaire |
| Statia Day, 16 November | – | public in Sint Eustatius |
| Saba Day, first Friday of December | – | public in Saba |

Not modelled: Easter Sunday and Whit Sunday (Sundays, D7); the day after the carnival parade (Art. 23 b), because the parade date is set each year on the island; any decision of an island council to move a day (Art. 23 para. 3). Art. 23 gives no name for 1 July. The data calls it “Emancipatiedag” / “Emancipation Day” after the abolition of slavery on 1 July 1863; the name is not taken from an official source (open point).

## Differences with OpenHolidays

| Difference | Who is right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ours only: Labour Day, Rincon Day, Emancipation Day, Dia di Himno y Bandera, Statia Day, Saba Day in the Caribbean Netherlands | ours | Arbeidswet 2000 BES, Art. 23. OpenHolidays has no BES days. |
| Theirs only: Easter Sunday, Whit Sunday | both | Sundays are not listed (D7). |

The comparison looks at dates, not types. OpenHolidays has Good Friday, Liberation Day and Whit Monday as nationwide `Public` days. Ours are `observance` for Good Friday and Liberation Day in the European Netherlands and for Liberation Day and Whit Monday in the BES (D-bnl-2, D-bnl-3), so a default query returns fewer days than OpenHolidays.

## Checked by hand

2025, 2026 and 2027 were recomputed independently from the texts above (a separate script) and compared with `json/nl/`: no difference. The Rijksoverheid dates for 2026 and 2027 match (King’s Day 27 April in both years; in 2025 it was Saturday 26 April). Queen’s Day 2000 and 2006 are 29 April; Labour Day in the BES is 2 May in 2011 and 2022.

## Open points

- The Royal Decree of 24 April 1980 (Queen’s Day) was not found. The Saturday rule for 2000 and 2006 rests on the two Staatscourant notices.
- Dia di Rincon: Art. 23 j names the day of the celebration without a date; 30 April is from the civil-service regulation.
- Carnival day in BES (Saba now; Bonaire until 2019, Sint Eustatius until 2021): no rule for the date. A decree table per year (`years` with `list`) could add it once the dates are collected.
- The name of 1 July in Sint Eustatius (see above).
- Papiamentu (Bonaire) and English (Saba, Sint Eustatius) are not in `languages`; adding them needs a name in each for every holiday.
