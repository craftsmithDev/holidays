# France: sources and verification

## Sources

No reference dataset exists for France, so every day was researched from the law:

1. **Code du travail L3133-1** ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000033020901)): the eleven fêtes légales. The list is the same in the version of 1 May 2008 to 10 August 2016 (read on Légifrance) and in the current one; before 2008 it was article L222-1 of the old numbering (recodified by ordonnance n° 2007-329). 8 May has been on the list since 1981, so the whole range 2000–2050 uses one list.
2. **Code du travail L3133-4**: 1 May is the only compulsory day off nationwide. **L3133-11**: the journée de solidarité may be put on a former jour férié other than 1 May.
3. **Code du travail L3134-13** ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006902635), text also read on code.travail.gouv.fr): in Moselle, Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin every jour férié is a day off, plus Good Friday “dans les communes ayant un temple protestant ou une église mixte” and the second day of Christmas. The DREETS Grand Est guide to local labour law (December 2022) says the same.
4. **Abolition of slavery overseas.** Loi n° 83-550 (Guadeloupe, Guyane, Martinique, La Réunion, Mayotte) and décret n° 83-1003 art. 1er, which sets the dates; décret n° 2012-553 added Saint-Barthélemy (9 October) and Saint-Martin (27 May) from 26 April 2012. Since 10 August 2016 the days are also in Code du travail L3422-2; Mayotte was added to that article on 1 January 2018, when the Code du travail started to apply there.
5. **Service-Public.gouv.fr**, “Jours fériés et ponts dans le secteur privé” (checked 1 January 2026): names of the days, the 2026 and 2027 tables, the Alsace-Moselle and overseas lists.
6. **Cross-check, not a source**: OpenHolidays API and date-holidays.

Decisions taken for France: D-fr-1 (regions), D-fr-2 (what `public` means, Whit Monday), D-fr-3 (Good Friday in Alsace-Moselle), in [decisions.md](../decisions.md).

## Regions

ISO 3166-2:FR was checked against its change history: the 2016 regions, FR-971 to FR-976 since 2020, FR-6AE (Alsace) and FR-75C since 2021, and the removal of FR-GP, FR-MQ, FR-GF, FR-RE and FR-YT in 2021. The data lists FR-57, FR-67, FR-68, FR-971, FR-972, FR-973, FR-974, FR-976, FR-BL and FR-MF (D-fr-1).

The comparison script now maps codes for both external datasets: OpenHolidays uses its own subdivision codes (FR-GE-MO for Moselle), which are translated through its `isoCode` field; date-holidays knows the overseas departments as GP, MQ, GF, RE and YT. Without that mapping every regional day showed up as a false difference. The CH and LI reports are unchanged by it.

## Hand check 2025–2027 (second pass)

Every date of 2025, 2026 and 2027 was compared per region with the year files:

- Nationwide days 2026 and 2027 against the Service-Public tables: 1 January, Easter Monday (6 April 2026, 29 March 2027), 1 May, 8 May, Ascension (14 May 2026, 6 May 2027), Whit Monday (25 May 2026, 17 May 2027), 14 July, 15 August, 1 November, 11 November, 25 December. 2025 against the Easter dates (Easter 20 April 2025: Easter Monday 21 April, Ascension 29 May, Whit Monday 9 June). All match.
- Alsace-Moselle: Good Friday 18 April 2025, 3 April 2026, 26 March 2027 (Service-Public gives 3 April 2026 and 26 March 2027); 26 December every year. All match.
- Overseas, each year: Mayotte 27 April, Martinique 22 May, Guadeloupe and Saint-Martin 27 May, Guyane 10 June, Saint-Barthélemy 9 October, La Réunion 20 December (L3422-2, décret n° 83-1003, Service-Public). All match.

No difference was found, so nothing was changed in the second pass.

## Open points

- **Good Friday in Alsace-Moselle** is an `observance` (D-fr-3). If the owner prefers the common practice (most calendars and many employers treat it as a holiday in the whole of the three departments), it becomes `public` with a note; the law text supports the current choice.
- **Saint-Martin in 2017.** The first version of L3422-2, in force between 10 August 2016 and the end of 2017, read “28 mars” for Saint-Martin, while décret n° 83-1003 kept 27 May. The Soualiga Post of 26 May 2017 reports 27 May 2017 as the holiday and the March date as an uncorrected error. The data has 27 May in 2017; 28 March 2017 is not listed.
- **Mayotte before 2018.** The Code du travail applicable à Mayotte (former art. L222-1) governed the holidays until 31 December 2017. Secondary sources (legisocial.fr) say it listed 27 April and Whit Monday; its full list was not read on Légifrance. The data assumes the national list applied there as well.
- **Local days overseas** (Victor Schœlcher Day, Fête de Saint-Barthélemy, carnival days, Mi-Carême): no legal source found; not listed.
- **Collectivities with their own labour law** (French Polynesia, New Caledonia, Wallis and Futuna) are not covered (D-fr-1).
